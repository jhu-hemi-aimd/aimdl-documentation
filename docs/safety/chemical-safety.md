# Chemical handling and waste

This page covers labeling, chemical use, storage, and waste handling in AIMD-L.
Use it with the [SDS index](sds.md), [PPE requirements](ppe.md), and the
applicable equipment or experiment SOP.

    This page is awaiting lab safety review and is not yet approved for
    operational use. Follow current JHU policy, posted laboratory controls,
    required training, and instructions from AIMD-L staff.

## Labeling samples, chemicals, waste, and active experiments

Clear labels identify the material, its owner, and its hazards. A harmless-looking
liquid may be water or a hazardous chemical. Label all containers, including
temporary containers, with at least:

- Contents, including the full chemical name and sample description when applicable.
- Researcher name or responsible owner.
- Date labeled or prepared.
- Expiration date, if applicable.
- Hazards or special handling notes when relevant.

Label active experiments so other researchers know not to disturb the setup and
can contact the responsible user if a problem is suspected.

## Chemical PPE and use

- Review the chemical's SDS and the applicable SOP before use.
- Follow the [laboratory PPE requirements](ppe.md), including chemical-specific glove selection.
- Use splash goggles when splash exposure is possible. Use a face shield when required by the chemical, procedure, or SOP.
- Use chemicals only in the fume hood or another approved ventilated area unless the SOP states otherwise.
- Lower the fume hood sash to the indicated position during work.
- Make sure reactive or incompatible materials are not present in the hood before beginning work.
- Use the smallest practical quantity of chemical for the task.

## Chemical storage

- Store acids, bases, solvents, and flammables in the appropriate marked storage cabinets.
- Do not store acids and bases together or store other incompatible chemicals together.
- Label all containers, even when used temporarily.
- Store only flammables in the flammables cabinet.
- Corrosives generally are not permitted in AIMD-L unless specifically approved and stored appropriately.

Listing a chemical in the SDS index does not establish authorization to use or
store it. Confirm applicable approvals and storage requirements with AIMD-L staff.

## Chemical waste and disposal

Users are responsible for labeling chemical waste, keeping incompatible wastes
separate, and placing waste in the designated satellite accumulation area.
AIMD-L staff coordinate final disposal or pickup.

The laboratory's chemical-waste satellite accumulation area is **in the fume
hood in the G30C specimen preparation room**.

- Use appropriate waste containers for solvents, acids, bases, and other chemical waste streams.
- Do not mix incompatible chemicals, such as organic solvents with oxidizers.
- Label every chemical-waste container with the provided chemical-waste tags and keep it in the designated satellite accumulation area.
- Consult the [JHU OES Chemical Storage, Segregation & Incompatibility guidance](https://oes.jhu.edu/chemical-safety/chemical-storage-segregation-incompatibility/) and the product-specific SDS before combining or disposing of unfamiliar materials.
- Coordinate chemical disposal with AIMD-L engineers.

Use the [JHU OES Chemical and Hazardous Material Waste Disposal guidance](https://oes.jhu.edu/services-and-resources/chemical-and-hazardous-material-waste-disposal/)
for current disposal arrangements and the linked **Chemical Waste Request form**.
Coordinate the request with the lab engineers. OES instructs requestors to ensure
waste is tagged and to provide entry instructions in the form's "Requestor
Comments" field if the lab is keyed. Consult the OES page for current pickup and
drop-off arrangements rather than relying on a copied schedule.

## Biohazard boxes

The red-bag biobox practice applies to AIMD-L. Use these boxes for the designated
laboratory and biological waste streams. They are not for food, drink waste,
ordinary trash, or recyclable material that is not contaminated with laboratory
waste. Coordinate chemical waste through the separate chemical-disposal process
above; consult the lab engineers if the correct waste stream is unclear.

1. Seal new boxes along all three bottom seams with transparent poly tape.
2. Do not overfill boxes. Close and dispose of them when approximately two-thirds full.
3. Twist and tape the bag closed, then seal the top flaps along all three seams.
4. Label the box with the PI name, building, room number, and contact extension.
5. Place the sealed and labeled box in the **biobox accumulation area. This is in the main corridor outside of the AIMDL, between the cylinder storage and the lavatories.** Sealed and labeled boxes may be stacked in this area for pickup.

New biobox supplies are also available at this accumulation area.
