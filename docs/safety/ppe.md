# Attire and Personal Protective Equipment

## Required Lab Attire

- Closed-toe shoes are required.
- Long pants are required.
- Avoid loose or baggy clothing.
- Avoid synthetic materials that can easily melt or catch fire, such as polyester, when laser or hot-work hazards are present.
- Hair below the shoulder must be tied back.

The corridor outside the control room contains racks with shared PPE and
lockers for AIMD-L users.

## Eye Protection

- Clear safety glasses must be worn in the primary lab space and connected laboratory spaces.
- Safety glasses provided by AIMD-L are located on wire racks in the corridor outside the control room.
- Users may wear their own safety glasses if they are certified ANSI Z87.1.
- Appropriately rated laser safety glasses or goggles must be worn in the laser enclosure, when using the OMTECH laser, or whenever a Class 1 laser condition is not assured. Laser eyewear may be worn in place of clear safety glasses when it provides the required protection.

## Lab Coats

- A flame-resistant lab coat is required in the lab space.
- AIMD-L-provided lab coats are located in the corridor outside the control room.
- Users may provide their own lab coat or jumpsuit if it is flame- and chemical-resistant.
- Use the laundering bin for coats that are soiled, damaged, or due for laundering. Regular users should plan to use one coat for the week unless it becomes soiled or damaged.

## Gloves

- Nitrile gloves are standard for general laboratory work and for preventing specimen or optical-part contamination.
- Nitrile gloves are not suitable for prolonged contact with solvents, oxidizers, or acids. Use chemically compatible heavy rubber gloves when needed.
- Before handling unfamiliar or higher-hazard chemicals, review the chemical's SDS and the chemical-resistance guide for the exact glove product being used. The JHU Chemistry Student Safety Committee's [glove-selection guide](https://sites.krieger.jhu.edu/chemssc/files/2023/03/Safety-Moment-glove-selection-March-2023.pdf) and DMSE's [Chemical Gloves presentation](https://engineering.jhu.edu/materials/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Glove-details_4sept2019_Dan_Kuespert.pdf) explain how glove material, thickness, chemical concentration, exposure time, and manufacturer affect compatibility.
- Leather or thick cut-resistant gloves are appropriate for handling sharp materials, workpieces, or hot laser-processed materials.
- **Do not wear gloves when working with rotating power tools.**
- Do not wear nitrile gloves outside the lab. Dispose of contaminated gloves in the appropriate lab waste container before leaving. Transport specimens in a secondary container rather than carrying them in gloved hands outside the lab.

## Respiratory and Hearing Protection

- Wear an N95 dust mask or other specified respiratory protection when an SOP requires it, including work with powders or materials whose particles may be inhaled during handling.
- Ear plugs or protective headphones should be worn when working with pressurized gas or during re-pressurization, cylinder reconnection, or gas-line modification. Ear protection is provided in the corridor.
