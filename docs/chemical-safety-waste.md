# Chemical Handling, Labeling, Waste, and Biohazard Boxes

## Labeling samples, chemicals, waste, and active experiments

Clear labeling allows other users to understand what materials are present, who is responsible for them, and whether they pose a hazard. Labeling is especially important for liquids, because a harmless-looking liquid may be water or may be a hazardous chemical.

Labels must include, at minimum:

- Contents, including full chemical name and sample description when applicable.
- Researcher name or responsible owner.
- Date labeled or prepared.
- Expiration date, if applicable.
- Hazards or special handling notes when relevant.

Active experiments must also be labeled so other researchers do not disturb the setup and can contact the responsible user if a problem is suspected.

## Chemical PPE and use

- Use splash goggles when splash exposure is possible. Use a face shield when required by the chemical, procedure, or SOP.
- Use chemicals only in the fume hood or other approved ventilated area unless the SOP states otherwise.
- Lower the fume hood sash to the indicated position during work.
- Make sure reactive or incompatible materials are not present in the hood before beginning work.
- Use the smallest practical quantity of chemical for the task.

## Chemical storage

- Store acids, bases, solvents, and flammables in the appropriate marked storage cabinets.
- Do not store acids and bases together.
- Do not store incompatible chemicals together.
- All containers must be labeled, even if they are used temporarily.
- Only flammables should be stored in the flammables cabinet. Corrosives generally are not permitted in AIMDL unless specifically approved and stored appropriately.

## Chemical waste and disposal

Users are responsible for labeling chemical waste, keeping incompatible wastes separate, and placing waste in the designated satellite accumulation area. AIMDL staff are responsible for coordinating final disposal or pickup.

- Use appropriate waste containers for solvents, acids, bases, and other chemical waste streams.
- Do not mix incompatible chemicals. Common incompatibilities, such as organic solvents with oxidizers, should be posted near the satellite accumulation area and fume hood.
- All chemical waste containers must be labeled with provided chemical-waste tags and kept in the designated satellite accumulation area.
- Consult the posted chemical compatibility reference and the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) before combining or disposing of unfamiliar materials.
- Coordinate chemical disposal with AIMDL engineers. HSE pickup or the chemical waste collection site may be used according to current JHU procedures.

## SDS and reference locations

Safety Data Sheets for chemicals used in AIMDL should be available through the AIMDL digital safety folder and, where practical, posted or linked near the fume hood and chemical storage area. Before final release, the document owner should insert the exact SDS repository location or QR code.

## Red biohazard boxes

JHU Health, Safety and Environment requires laboratories to maintain red-bag biohazard disposal boxes. In AIMDL, these boxes are for lab waste and biological material only. They are not for food, drink waste, ordinary trash, or recyclable material that is not contaminated with laboratory waste.

- Seal new boxes along all three bottom seams with transparent poly tape.
- Do not overfill boxes. Close and dispose of them when approximately two-thirds full.
- To close a box, twist and tape the bag closed, then seal the top flaps along all three seams.
- Label the box with the PI name, building, room number, and contact extension.
- Place the sealed and labeled box only at the designated temporary hallway storage location for vendor pickup.
